What “evidence-informed” means here

The assessment uses the RIASEC vocational-interest framework, the public-domain 20-item Mini-IPIP Big Five measure, six established work-value domains, and readiness prompts. Research finds that interest–program fit is useful for reflection, but only a small predictor of achievement, persistence, and satisfaction. For that reason, this tool does not treat any score as destiny.

The original career-interest, values, readiness, field-mapping, and recommendation system in this prototype has not undergone an independent validation study. Use it to make a shortlist and better questions—not a final enrolment decision.

Method & sources

What this tool can—and cannot—tell you

Interests. The original activity items are organized with the RIASEC model used in vocational psychology and by the O*NET® Interest Profiler. They are not the official O*NET items and this prototype is not an O*NET product.

Personality. The 20 public-domain IPIP items form the Mini-IPIP Big Five scales. Scores show a position on the response scale, not a percentile and not a fixed “type.” Personality describes tendencies; it does not decide a suitable degree.

Values and readiness. The six value domains are informed by the O*NET Work Importance Locator framework. The wording and short scales here are original and have not been independently validated. Readiness ratings are self-reflection prompts, not aptitude tests.

Study-family ranking. Each family has a documented target profile. The exploration score is 65% activity interests, 20% current readiness evidence, and 15% work values. Those weights are a transparent design heuristic, not a clinically or psychometrically validated prediction model. Personality is deliberately excluded from the ranking to reduce typecasting.
