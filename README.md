# Career Compass

Career Compass is a self-contained, private browser assessment for a 17-year-old exploring what to study at university.

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. No build step, account, server, or internet connection is required after the files have loaded. Answers are stored only in the browser's local storage and can be deleted with **Start over**.

## Assessment model

The assessment contains 77 items:

- 36 original activity-interest prompts organized using the RIASEC framework: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, and Conventional.
- 20 public-domain Mini-IPIP items measuring broad Big Five tendencies.
- 12 original reflection prompts across six work-value domains: Achievement, Independence, Recognition, Relationships, Support, and Working Conditions.
- 9 original self-ratings of current readiness evidence.

The study-family **exploration score** is a transparent heuristic:

```text
65% activity-interest match
20% current readiness evidence
15% work-value match
```

Personality scores are deliberately excluded from the study-family ranking. They are used only to suggest learning-environment questions, reducing the risk of typecasting a teenager into or out of a degree.

Scores are positions on a 1–5 response scale converted linearly to 0–100. They are not population percentiles.

## Evidence base

- [O*NET Interest Profiler Manual](https://www.onetcenter.org/reports/IP_Manual.html) — vocational-interest assessment and the RIASEC model.
- [Donnellan et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1037/1040-3590.18.2.192) — development and validation of the 20-item Mini-IPIP.
- [International Personality Item Pool](https://ipip.ori.org/) — source and public-domain status of the personality items.
- [de Vries, Meeter, & Huizinga (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2024.100619) — meta-analysis finding that interest–study-program congruence is a positive but small predictor of achievement, persistence, and satisfaction.
- [Vedel, Thomsen, & Larsen (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2015.04.030) — evidence that personality patterns and their relationships with performance vary across academic majors.
- [Porfeli (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2006.04.005) — longitudinal evidence that work-value systems develop during adolescence.

## Important limitations

This is an evidence-informed prototype, not a validated psychological instrument as a whole. The career-interest prompts, short values scales, readiness prompts, study-family target profiles, and weighting formula have not undergone independent psychometric validation.

The assessment must not be used to:

- diagnose a psychological condition;
- decide admissions, hiring, tracking, or eligibility;
- predict grades, income, job-market demand, or happiness;
- rule a student into or out of a subject.

It is designed to produce a shortlist and a research plan. A final decision should also consider actual grades, prerequisites, program content, accreditation, cost, location, language, disability access, family circumstances, and local labor-market information.
